# Docside First Look — Vision Brief

> **Source:** Morris's founding Google Doc, imported 2026-08-03 so the repo does not depend on an external link. Content preserved faithfully with light formatting cleanup; editor's notes are marked as such.

A **guided product-research experience**, not simply a landing page with a tour.

# Docside First Look

### A private preview for selected real estate professionals

The experience should accomplish three things:

1. Make the agent feel personally invited.
2. Let the agent experience Docside and provide candid evidence about what works, what confuses them, and what they do not yet trust.

## Strongest recommendation

Create a separate experience at a URL such as:

`preview.docside.ai/morris-invite-code`

The preview page would introduce the assignment, launch the agent into a controlled Docside workspace, accompany them through several missions, and collect feedback at important moments.

The experience should be approximately **10–15 minutes**. Unmoderated usability tests generally perform better when they remain within roughly 15–20 minutes, and onboarding checklists are usually most effective when they concentrate on three to five meaningful actions.

## 1. Personal welcome

The page should greet the agent by name:

> **Welcome, Angela.**
> Morris invited you to privately preview an early version of Docside.

Then explain why they were selected:

> You were invited because of your experience working with real estate purchase agreements and communicating offer terms to sellers. This is not a sales presentation. We would like your honest assessment of what Docside does well, what feels unclear, and what would make it more useful in your work.

Include three small expectations:

- Approximately 12 minutes
- No preparation required
- Honest criticism is encouraged

Use a button such as: **Begin My Docside Preview**

No account creation. No password. The personalized link should admit them directly.

## 2. Capture their first impression before explaining everything

Before showing the explainer video, briefly show Docside's main introductory screen and ask:

> **Based on what you see, what do you believe Docside does?**

This is extremely valuable. A five-second or first-impression test helps determine whether the product's purpose is understandable without a lengthy explanation. Compare it with what they say after using the application.

This answers an important question: **Can Docside explain itself, or does Morris have to explain it?**

## 3. A short founder video

After the first-impression question, show a personal video from Morris lasting approximately 45–60 seconds, covering:

- The problem observed
- What Docside is attempting to do
- What they will experience
- Why their candid feedback matters

Suggested message:

> Real estate agents receive some of the most important information in a transaction inside lengthy documents and email attachments. I created Docside to help make those documents easier to understand, verify, compare, and communicate.
>
> What you are about to see is an early working version—not the finished product. I would like you to approach it as though you have just received offers on one of your listings. Please use it naturally. Nothing you do will break it, and there are no wrong answers. The most helpful feedback is anything that surprises you, slows you down, or causes you not to trust what you see.

The video should not demonstrate every click. Otherwise, you will be testing whether they followed the video rather than whether Docside is understandable.

## 4. Give them a realistic assignment

Instead of saying "Explore Docside," place them inside a believable transaction scenario:

> **Your assignment**
> You are the listing agent for 1248 Oakview Drive. Three purchase offers have been received. Your seller would like help understanding the differences before deciding how to respond.

The preview workspace should already contain:

- A sample property
- Three carefully prepared sample offers
- Realistic but fictional buyer information
- Meaningful differences among price, financing, contingencies, deposit, closing date, and other relevant terms
- At least one unusual or easily overlooked provision

Do not initially ask agents to upload one of their own purchase agreements. That introduces privacy concerns, document selection, account setup, and the fear of making a mistake before they have experienced any value. At the end, you can offer an optional second experience using their own documents.

# The four missions

Use a persistent progress indicator: **Your Preview: 1 of 4**

Product onboarding guidance generally recommends concentrating on a small number of actions that lead users directly to the product's principal value rather than presenting every feature at once.

## Mission 1: Understand an offer

> Open the Anderson offer and determine its purchase price, financing type, deposit, and proposed closing date.

This introduces document understanding. Important: allow the agent to click a term and see where it came from in the original contract.

This should be Docside's first major trust moment:

> **Summary → extracted value → original source language**

Afterward, ask one lightweight question:

> How confident are you that you could verify where Docside found this information?

Use a 1–5 scale and an optional comment.

## Mission 2: Verify what Docside found

> Review the highlighted terms and confirm whether Docside represented them correctly.

Let the agent:

- Confirm a value
- Correct a value
- Mark something for further review
- Open the source page

This communicates that Docside does not expect the agent to accept an unexplained AI answer. It introduces the product's trust doctrine experientially rather than through marketing language.

## Mission 3: Compare the offers

> Your seller cares most about net proceeds, certainty, and closing within 30 days. Which offer would you discuss first?

The comparison view should display the offers side by side, while allowing the agent to inspect the reasoning behind any ordering or emphasis.

Do not tell them which offer Docside believes is "best" unless the ranking logic is fully traceable. The strongest Docside experience is not merely "Here is the top offer." It is:

> Here is how these offers differ, here are the seller's stated priorities, and here is the information behind the presentation.

## Mission 4: Prepare the seller conversation

> Preview the summary you might share with your seller. What would you change before sending it?

Allow them to inspect the live-link seller presentation, but make clear that this is a preview and nothing will actually be sent.

Ask: **What information would you add, remove, or explain differently?**

This is likely to uncover important differences between what agents need for themselves and what they are comfortable placing in front of sellers.

# The final feedback experience

Do not finish with one large generic survey asking "What did you think?" Use a short, carefully structured debrief.

| Part | Question | Format |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Understanding | Now that you have used it, how would you describe Docside to another real estate agent? | Open text (compare with first impression) |
| 2. Value | Which part of Docside felt most valuable? | Understanding an individual offer · Verifying contract terms · Comparing multiple offers · Preparing information for the seller · None yet |
| 3. Trust | Was there anything you saw that you would hesitate to rely upon? | Open text (more useful than "Did you trust Docside?") |
| 4. Workflow fit | At what point in your normal workflow could you see yourself using Docside? | First offer arrives · Multiple offers received · Before presenting to seller · During counteroffers · After acceptance · I do not yet see where it fits |
| 5. Friction | What was the most confusing or difficult part? | Open text |
| 6. Missing capability | What would Docside need before you would use it on an active listing? | Open text |
| 7. Commitment | Would you be willing to use Docside with one of your own offer packages? | Yes, now · Yes, after certain improvements · Possibly, but I need more information · No |
| 8. Open response | — | Type feedback · Record an audio response · Schedule a short conversation with Morris |

Some agents will explain far more naturally by speaking than by completing a text box.

# Make the ending memorable

After they submit feedback, show a personal thank-you page:

> **Thank you, Angela. You helped shape Docside.**
> Your feedback will be reviewed directly by Morris and used to determine what Docside should become next.

Then show a small summary: four activities completed · feedback submitted · Founding Preview Participant designation.

Optionally generate a tasteful digital card:

> **Docside Founding Agent Preview**
> Participant No. 007

This should feel appreciative, not gimmicky. A final optional choice:

- Try Docside with my own documents
- Join the next feedback round
- I am finished for now

# Personalization that would matter

- Agent's first name
- A note from Morris explaining why they were selected
- A personalized thank-you page
- A follow-up email referencing the portion they completed

Do **not** personalize the underlying product behavior differently per agent. Participants should experience the same core workflow so their feedback can be compared.

# Information to capture automatically

Beyond survey answers, record:

- Whether they watched the video
- Where they clicked first
- Time to complete each mission
- Which source-document links they open
- Corrections they attempted
- Hints requested
- Steps skipped
- Where they stopped
- Desktop or mobile usage
- Whether they returned later
- Whether they volunteered to use their own documents

Usability is fundamentally about whether people can accomplish their intended goals, so task completion and observed behavior should carry more weight than positive comments alone. Session recording could be valuable, provided the agent gives clear permission before recording begins.

# What not to build

- A five-minute explainer video
- A long product-feature presentation
- A ten-step guided tour
- Mandatory account creation
- Mandatory document uploading
- A survey consisting primarily of 1–10 ratings
- Leading questions such as "How helpful was the comparison?"
- Confetti, points, badges, or excessive animation
- Promises about features that are not present
- A polished sales pitch that makes participants reluctant to criticize
- A tour that places tooltips over every button

The experience should feel polished, but it should openly say:

> This is an early working version. Some portions are incomplete. Please evaluate what is present rather than what has been promised.

# The first version to build

The minimum worthwhile version contains seven screens:

1. Personalized welcome
2. First-impression question
3. Founder video
4. Transaction scenario
5. Four-mission Docside workspace
6. Feedback survey
7. Personalized thank-you and next-step page

The experience page should be separate from the normal Docside application, but it should **launch the real application in a controlled preview state**. Do not build a fake reproduction of Docside unless some portions of the actual application are too unstable to test.

# Recommended rollout

Begin with a small **Founding Agent Preview group**. For the earliest participants, observe them live over Zoom or in person while they share their screen and think aloud — moderated testing is particularly useful when detailed explanations and follow-up questions matter more than volume. After correcting the most obvious problems, send the self-guided experience to the next group.

> **Observe → correct → release → measure → interview**

# The central experience

The unforgettable moment should not be the video, animation, or personalized greeting. It should be this:

> The agent sees an important offer term, questions it, clicks it, and Docside immediately takes them to the exact place in the purchase agreement that supports it.

That moment communicates Docside's real promise:

> **Docside does not merely provide an answer. It helps the agent understand and verify the answer.**

The next useful deliverable is a complete product brief defining every screen, interaction, data point, feedback question, and acceptance criterion for the Docside First Look experience.
